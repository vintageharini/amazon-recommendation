How Amazon recommends a product

Every product is described by a set of features — category, attributes, who bought it, what else they bought. Recommending is mostly a similarity problem: given what you're looking at, which other items are described by the most overlapping features?

How the real thing works

  • Content-based filtering — comparing a product's own attributes (category, description, specs) to other products with similar attributes.
  • Collaborative filtering — comparing behavior: if many customers who bought A also bought B, B gets recommended to new A buyers, even if A and B look nothing alike on paper.
  • Ranking — candidates from both approaches are scored and blended, then re-ranked using your own browsing and purchase history.
  • Feedback loop — every click, purchase, and skipped recommendation becomes new training data for the next prediction.

This demo uses a simplified content-based approach: each product has a handful of tags, and similarity is measured by how many tags two products share. Amazon's real system blends this with collaborative signals from hundreds of millions of shoppers.
